Recommendation

A · Contour is the strongest base for the product.

  • Best before/after: the hero shows the same crest at 1×, 3× and 8×, and an Outlines view draws every traced path with its anchor points over the original pixels. It proves vector quality instead of claiming it.
  • Most complete professional workspace: files on the left, a large synchronized split / side-by-side / vector / outlines / original canvas, and an inspector with mode, cost, measured stats, palette and a pinned Download button.
  • Clearest credits and history: the balance appears in every app header, and the history table and credit ledger show exactly what each trace cost and when it will be deleted.
  • Light first suits color work: print shops judge colors on light paper; the dark theme is equally complete.
  • Easiest to build well: conventional, accessible layout primitives that map cleanly onto the existing React and Tailwind app.

Worth borrowing whichever you pick: B’s loupe as an extra demo on the landing page and B’s ⌘K command palette in the workspace; C’s “Which mode is right?” chooser and step-one tips for first-time users. Nightshift is the most striking, but its floating, canvas-only interface hides credits and stats and is harder on phones; Naqsh is the friendliest and most local, but feels less like a professional tool.

Concept A

Contour: precision light

Open the prototype

A technical specimen sheet for designers, print shops and sign makers. Light by default. Process cyan marks interaction, process magenta marks vector geometry, and a CMYK registration bar signs the footer. The workspace follows desktop design tools: files, canvas, inspector.

Type
Geologica (display), Golos Text (text), JetBrains Mono (data). All three include Cyrillic.
Color
Signature
Outlines view: anchor points over the original pixels, at any zoom.

Strengths

  • Proof of quality is built into the page: zoom levels, outlines, measured numbers.
  • Most complete tool: every action and number visible at once.
  • Highest contrast, conventional semantics, easy to maintain.

Trade-offs

  • Less dramatic than B on first impression.
  • The three-pane layout needs simplifying on phones (done: file strip, stacked inspector).

Concept B

Nightshift: dark studio

Open the prototype

A darkroom: near-black studio, artwork on lit light-table plates, one safelight amber for actions. The landing page is a stage with a loupe. The workspace is all canvas, with floating controls, a filmstrip and a ⌘K command palette for keyboard users.

Type
Spectral (display serif, italic accent), Wix Madefor Text (UI), IBM Plex Mono (data). All three include Cyrillic.
Color
Signature
The loupe: the uploaded pixels and the vector side by side under your cursor or finger.

Strengths

  • The most premium, cinematic first impression.
  • Largest preview: the image owns the screen.
  • Keyboard-first: ⌘K, zoom and pan keys.

Trade-offs

  • Floating panels hide stats and credits until opened, especially on phones.
  • Dark by default is less natural for judging print colors (the plates stay white).
  • The loupe needs the full original upload (534 KB here), loaded after the page.

Concept C

Naqsh: guided, crafted color

Open the prototype

Rishtan porcelain: white glaze, cobalt ink, turquoise and saffron. The 8-point star (khatam) and girih tiles are drawn as vector geometry, the product’s own subject. The workspace is a guided flow (Upload → Mode → Result) for print shops and people who trace a logo once a month.

Type
Unbounded (display), Onest (text). Both include Cyrillic.
Color
Signature
The “Which mode?” chooser, and a workspace that never leaves you guessing about the next step.

Strengths

  • The clearest path for first-time and occasional users.
  • A distinctive identity rooted in Uzbekistan, without stereotypes.
  • Best on phones.

Trade-offs

  • Heavier steps for professionals who trace in bulk.
  • A strong color identity is harder to keep restrained across a growing product.

Patterns studied, not copied

Vectorizer.AI

A zoomable result preview with single and side-by-side views and pan, and a format choice at download (vectorizer.ai; the site was not reachable from this environment, so this comes from its public descriptions). Used: synchronized zoom and pan across split and side-by-side views; the result is always inspectable before download.

Adobe Illustrator, Image Trace

View modes: tracing result, outlines, outlines with source, source image. Used: the Outlines view (paths and anchor points over the original) and the Original view, so users can judge how closely curves follow the source.

Figma

The canvas comes first, with minimal floating toolbars and properties in a side panel. Used: A’s inspector, B’s floating toolbar and canvas, pinch and scroll zoom.

Linear

A command menu, keyboard-first work and restrained color. Used: B’s ⌘K palette, keyboard zoom and pan on every canvas, one accent per concept.

No logos, layouts, copy or color schemes were taken from these products.

What is real

  • Every result image is a Vectorla trace made by the production pipeline at commit b548a69, in both modes, from nine samples: Vectorla benchmark test designs, Microsoft Fluent Emoji (MIT license) and scikit-image sample photos (CC0). The customer emblem from the engine rounds is not used: it is licensed for benchmarking only. Regenerate with design/concepts/tools/trace-samples.ts.
  • Every number (shapes, colors, gradients, SVG size, trace time) is measured from those traces on the benchmark machine.
  • Product facts match the backend: Quick 1 credit, Professional 2, 10 free credits, PNG/JPG/WebP up to 5 MB, SVG output, deletion after 30 days, automatic refunds, and switching modes refunds the earlier trace. Paid plans are shown as “coming soon”, with no prices.
  • Example data: the history and credit ledger use the real traces, but their dates and the balance (2 of 10) are illustrative and labeled as example entries. There are no testimonials, ratings, customer logos or usage claims.
  • Prototype uploads stay in the browser tab and are never sent anywhere. The page says so and offers samples instead.
